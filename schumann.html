🚀 High-Altitude Rocketry Payload Connection

Our student rocketry research team utilizes Schumann Resonances to study lower ionosphere D-layer boundary fluctuations. As high-power payloads ascend through lower atmospheric boundaries, onboard magnetometers and ELF telemetry receptors collect meteorological and electromagnetic background metrics.

Mission Goal: Correlating atmospheric pressure profiles with low-frequency radio propagation during sounding rocket apogee flight trajectories.

Understanding Schumann Resonances

Schumann Resonances are global electromagnetic resonances excited by worldwide lightning discharges in the natural cavity formed between Earth's surface and the ionosphere (~60–100 km altitude).

  • 1st Mode (Fundamental): 7.83 Hz (The primary cavity standing wave)
  • 2nd Mode: 14.3 Hz
  • 3rd Mode: 20.8 Hz
  • 4th Mode: 27.3 Hz
  • 5th Mode: 33.8 Hz

Changes in amplitude or transient bursts (bright horizontal bursts on the spectrogram) indicate intense global thunderstorm activity, solar flares, or solar wind disruptions impacting the ionosphere.
